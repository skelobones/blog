As I should've stated previously, AIF needs students to know what they want to learn. *How* we learn is important though. VARK is an acronym for Visual-Aural-Readwrite-Kinesthetic. These are different categories of learning. We were instructued to do a self-assessed [survey](https://vark-learn.com/the-vark-questionnaire/) to find our VARK orientation. 

Visual learning may be; I want to watch a YouTube video on safe manual handling to see how someone else did it.
Aural learning may be; Listening to a teacher's lecture and conversating with the teacher (or a study buddy) about it.
Read/write is the (my) stereotypical idea of study; self explanatory. Reading then/or writing notes.
Kinesthetic learning is 'hands on' education. Not necessarily 'experience' but they go hand-in-hand. It could also be receiving tangible information; a puppet show or a demonstration. (The latter joins with visual but nonetheless)

*This may be a short and uninformative log but it is a log nonetheless. Small steppy is better than no steppy.*
